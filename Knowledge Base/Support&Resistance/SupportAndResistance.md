#  Support & Resistance Analysis

## 1. What It Is

**Support and Resistance (S/R)** are key price zones on a chart where buying or selling interest is concentrated, temporarily pausing or reversing price trends.

## 2. How It Works

- **Support:** A price floor where demand is strong enough to prevent price from falling further.
- **Resistance:** A price ceiling where supply is strong enough to prevent price from rising further.

When a support or resistance zone breaks, market roles flip via **Change in Polarity (S/R Flip)**: broken resistance becomes new support, and broken support becomes new resistance.

## 3. Formulas

1. **Dynamic Support/Resistance (EMA):** $$Dynamic\ Level_t = EMA_N(Close_t)$$
    
2. **Pivot Point Standard Calculation:** $$Pivot\ (P) = \frac{High + Low + Close}{3}$$ $$Resistance\ 1\ (R1) = (2 \times P) - Low$$ $$Support\ 1\ (S1) = (2 \times P) - High$$
    

## 4. S/R Reference Points & Polarity Mechanics

**Change in Polarity (S/R Flip):** Price approaches a resistance level from below and breaks through it. On the subsequent pullback, price retests that same level from above — the old resistance now acts as new support before price continues higher.

|S/R Type|Reference Point Examples|Characteristics|
|---|---|---|
|Horizontal Zones|Prior reversal swing highs/lows, gap up/down boundaries|Strongest structural zones; institutional memory.|
|Psychological Levels|Key round numbers (e.g., 100, 500, 1000)|Concentrated cluster of retail/institutional limit orders.|
|Dynamic Levels|Moving Averages (20 EMA, 50 EMA, 200 EMA)|Shifts continuously as price evolves.|
|Intraday Anchors|Previous Day High (PDH), Previous Day Low (PDL)|Essential operational levels for day traders.|

## 5. Strengths

- **Foundational Framework:** Forms the backbone of technical trading, order placement, and risk management.
- **High Probability Execution:** Trading at key S/R zones offers tight stop-loss placement and high Risk-to-Reward ratios.
- **S/R Flip Reliability:** Change-in-polarity setups are among the most reliable price action setups in technical analysis.

## 6. Weaknesses

- **Not Thin Lines:** Treating S/R as exact single price lines rather than broad zones leads to premature stop-outs.
- **Zone Degradation:** The more frequently an S/R level is tested, the weaker it becomes as limit orders get filled.
- **Subjectivity:** Beginners often draw too many levels, creating cluttered charts and execution paralysis.

## 7. Best Practices

- **Draw Zones, Not Lines:** Always plot S/R as horizontal rectangular bands to accommodate price wicks.
- **Trade S/R Flips:** Look for entries when broken resistance is retested from above as new support.
- **Prioritize Higher Timeframes:** Weekly and daily S/R zones carry significantly more weight than 5-minute levels.

## 8. Common Mistakes

- **Assuming Lines Are Unbreakable:** Expecting support or resistance to hold indefinitely without confirming price action signals.
- **Placing Stops Directly on Levels:** Placing stop-loss orders exactly on the line where market makers frequently sweep liquidity.
- **Over-Drawing Charts:** Stacking dozens of lines on a single chart, creating analysis paralysis.
