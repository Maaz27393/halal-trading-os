#  Chart Patterns

## 1. What It Is

**Chart Patterns** are distinct geometric formations created by price movements on a chart. They represent consolidation, distribution, or accumulation phases driven by the ongoing battle between buyers and sellers.

## 2. How It Works

Chart patterns group market psychology into recognizable shapes that signal either a continuation of the prior trend or an impending market reversal. Traders identify pattern boundaries using trendlines (support and resistance) and execute trades when price breaks through these structural bounds, usually confirmed by increased volume.

## 3. Formulas

Target prices for chart patterns are calculated using the **Measured Move Principle**:

$$Pattern\ Height = |Pattern\ High - Pattern\ Low|$$ $$Bullish\ Breakout\ Target = Breakout\ Price\ Level + Pattern\ Height$$ $$Bearish\ Breakout\ Target = Breakout\ Price\ Level - Pattern\ Height$$

## 4. Key Attributes & Pattern Classifications

**Reversal Patterns:** Double Top (M-shape, bearish reversal) · Double Bottom (W-shape, bullish reversal) · Head & Shoulders (bearish reversal) / Inverse Head & Shoulders (bullish reversal)

**Continuation & Reversal Patterns:** Ascending Triangle (bullish breakout) · Descending Triangle (bearish breakout) · Rising Wedge (bearish) / Falling Wedge (bullish)

|Pattern Category|Patterns Included|Expected Direction|Key Action / Rule|
|---|---|---|---|
|Triangles|Ascending, Descending, Symmetrical|Ascending = Bullish, Descending = Bearish, Symmetrical = Dual Breakout|Trade breakout direction confirmed by volume expansion.|
|Wedges|Rising Wedge, Falling Wedge|Rising = Bearish Reversal, Falling = Bullish Reversal|Look for trend exhaustion near wedge apex.|
|Tops & Bottoms|Double Top (M-Shape), Double Bottom (W-Shape)|M-Shape = Bearish, W-Shape = Bullish|Enter on neckline breakout or retest.|
|Head & Shoulders|Standard H&S, Inverse H&S|Standard = Bearish Reversal, Inverse = Bullish Reversal|Execute on neckline break.|

## 5. Strengths

- **Objective Targets:** Provides mathematically calculated price objectives via pattern height projections.
- **Structural Clarity:** Clearly defines invalidation levels (stop losses) directly behind pattern boundaries.
- **Universal Application:** Applicable across all assets (stocks, forex, commodities) and timeframes.

## 6. Weaknesses

- **Subjectivity in Drawing:** Different traders may draw trendlines through candle wicks versus candle bodies, creating conflicting levels.
- **Fakeouts:** High incidence of false breakouts during low-volume market environments.
- **Lagging Completion:** Large patterns can take weeks or months to form, reducing trade frequency.

## 7. Best Practices

- **Require Retest Confirmation:** Wait for price to break out and retest the broken pattern boundary as new support/resistance before entering.
- **Filter with Volume:** Validate every pattern breakout with a spike in volume.
- **Align with Higher Timeframe:** Only trade continuation patterns that align with the main trend on weekly/daily charts.

## 8. Common Mistakes

- **Anticipating the Breakout:** Entering a trade inside the pattern _before_ the actual breakout occurs.
- **Ignoring Context:** Trading chart patterns in isolation without checking key horizontal support/resistance zones.
- **Forgetting Timeframe Scaling:** Expecting a 5-minute chart pattern breakout to yield a multi-week swing move.
