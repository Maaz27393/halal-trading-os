#  Institutional & Shariah Concepts

## 1. What It Is

**Institutional Concepts** explain the trading operations, order flow mechanics, manipulation risks (e.g., Front Running, Pump & Dump), and ethical/Shariah compliance frameworks that govern large-scale institutional participation.

## 2. How It Works

Institutional market players (FIIs, DIIs, Mutual Funds) handle massive capital reserves. Because they cannot enter positions instantly without causing massive price slippage, they absorb liquidity through accumulation, distribution, and market manipulation tactics. Additionally, ethical and **Shariah Compliance** frameworks establish strict parameters to eliminate exploitation (Riba), gambling (Maysir), and excessive risk/ambiguity (Gharar).

## 3. Formulas & Compliance Screening Metrics

1. **Impure Income Ratio (IR Ratio):** $$IR\ Ratio = \left(\frac{Non\text{-}Compliant\ /\ Other\ Income}{Total\ Revenue}\right) \times 100 \le 5%$$
    
2. **Total Debt-to-Market Cap Ratio:** $$Debt\ Ratio = \left(\frac{Total\ Interest\text{-}Bearing\ Debt}{Market\ Capitalization}\right) \times 100 < 33%$$
    
3. **Liquid Assets Ratio:** $$Illiquid\ Assets\ Ratio = \frac{Illiquid\ Assets}{Total\ Assets} > 50%$$
    

_(This third screen confirms the company holds sufficient illiquid/operating assets — property, plant, inventory — rather than being a pass-through cash or receivables vehicle, which matters for Shariah asset-backing requirements.)_

## 4. Market Manipulation & Compliance Rules

**Manipulation Schemes:**

1. **Front Running:** Insider sees a large pending order → buys for personal account → customer order executes → insider sells into the resulting move. _(Illegal)_
2. **Pump and Dump:** Insider hypes a stock → retail buys → price rockets → insider dumps holdings → price crashes. _(Illegal)_

|Screening Filter / Concept|Rule Standard|Purpose / Regulatory Rationale|
|---|---|---|
|Market Cap Threshold|Minimum ₹2,000 Crore|Filters out micro-cap stocks vulnerable to Pump & Dump schemes.|
|Minimum CMP|Share Price > ₹500|Avoids highly volatile penny stocks.|
|ROE Check|Return on Equity > 11%|Assures management efficiency and business stability.|
|Prohibited Activities|No Margin/Leverage, Short Selling, Derivatives (F&O), Forex, or Unscreened Crypto|Prevents Riba (Interest), Maysir (Gambling), and Gharar (Excessive Ambiguity).|
|Screening Tools|Islamicly App, Islamic Tijarat, AAOIFI Screeners|Verifies real-time Shariah compliance status of equities.|

## 5. Strengths

- **Protects Capital from Fraud:** Screening out illiquid penny stocks avoids institutional manipulation traps.
- **Ethical Trading Standards:** Eliminates high-risk speculative gambling (F&O) in favor of asset-backed spot trading.
- **Institutional Alignment:** Tracks FII/DII footprint to trade alongside institutional liquidity flows.

## 6. Weaknesses

- **Excludes Leveraged Products:** Strict Shariah compliance excludes high-leverage derivatives and short-selling options.
- **Requires Active Auditing:** Shariah financial ratios shift quarterly as earnings reports are released.
- **Smaller Investment Universe:** Filtering out non-compliant financial and debt-heavy sectors reduces total available stocks.

## 7. Best Practices

- **Use Dedicated Screening Applications:** Verify stocks using tools like Islamicly or Islamic Tijarat before technical analysis.
- **Focus on Cash/Spot Equity:** Execute long-only spot trades without margin borrowing or interest fees.
- **Track FII/DII Data:** Monitor daily institutional buying/selling metrics published by stock exchanges (NSE/BSE).

## 8. Common Mistakes

- **Buying Low-Volume Penny Stocks:** Falling prey to Pump & Dump schemes promoted on messaging channels.
- **Using Brokerage Leverage:** Trading on margin, introducing Riba (interest fees) and liquidation risk.
- **Ignoring Corporate Financial Statements:** Failing to re-check debt ratios quarterly to ensure ongoing compliance.
